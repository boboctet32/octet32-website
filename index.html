The problem

Address management is broken at the root

Most organizations manage IP addresses one at a time, reactively, in tools that were never designed for the scale and complexity of modern networks. The consequences show up as downtime, not tickets.

01

Conflicts discovered during outages

Duplicate or overlapping address allocations surface during routing failures, not before. By then the damage is done.

02

Lost address space

Allocated blocks that are never tracked, never reclaimed, and never reused quietly waste capacity that organizations pay for.

03

M&A integration nightmares

Merging two enterprise address spaces with no systematic block-level overlap detection means months of manual reconciliation.

04

IPv4 to IPv6 transition complexity

Managing dual-stack environments simultaneously requires tools designed for a single protocol to do the job of two.

05

Spreadsheet dependency

The de-facto standard for IPALM in most organizations — with no validation, no conflict prevention, and no hierarchy.

06

Security blind spots

Untracked addresses that appear in the network without audit trails create vulnerability surface that security teams cannot see.

The platform

Block-level management. Prevention first.

Octet32 acts as a Local Internet Registry within your environment — receiving upstream allocations and managing block delegation downward through your network hierarchy with correctness enforced at every step.

Aggregate tree visualization

A hierarchical view of your entire address space, organized by the topology that matches your organization — Region, Site, VLAN, host subnet — with utilization inline on every node.

Hard conflict prevention

Invalid allocations are rejected at input. No warnings that allow an operator to proceed with bad data. Correct by construction means the database never contains a conflict.

Auto-suggest next available subnet

Request a sub-block and the system finds the next available gap in the parent — filling holes before extending sequentially, with nibble-boundary alignment for IPv6.

Recursive utilization calculation

Utilization is calculated for any node in the tree — total addresses, allocated, available, and reserved — with /64 subnet counts as the primary metric for IPv6 planning.

IPv4 and IPv6 dual-stack native

Public, private, and ARIN-allocated address types managed in a single system. RFC 1918 boundaries enforced. Multiple routing domains supported with no cross-domain conflicts permitted.

Role-based access control

Scope, function, and block-size limits per user. A site admin can manage their subnet without touching regional allocations. Audit logging on every write.
